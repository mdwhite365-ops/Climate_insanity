Issues

Global Issues

Plastic pollution is a global issue affecting marine life and ecosystems. The used plastics are breaking down into microplastics, which pose a significant threat to marine life and ecosystems.Along with the over filling of landfills, this has become a major climate crisis.

Local: SF Bay

The San Francisco Bay Area, a hub of innovation and sustainability, is unfortunately plagued by significant plastic issues. Plastic waste is a major concern, with millions of plastic bottles, bags, and other items littering the region's beaches, waterways, and streets. The Bay Area's notorious traffic congestion and poor waste management infrastructure contribute to the problem, with many recyclables and trash ending up in landfills or as litter. According to a recent study, the Bay Area generates over 300,000 tons of plastic waste each year, with a significant portion of that waste ending up in local waterways, harming marine life and ecosystems. Additionally, microplastics, tiny plastic particles that are ingested by humans and wildlife, are a growing concern, with research suggesting that they can enter the food chain and cause harm to both humans and animals.

Studies

A new study from SFEI assessing the ability of rain gardens to remove contaminants from urban stormwater has shown that nature-based filtration may also be a solution for microplastic pollution.

Based on data collected between 2012 and 2017, polychlorinated biphenyls (PCBs) and suspended sediment were reduced by over 90%, whereas total mercury, methylmercury, and copper were moderately captured.
